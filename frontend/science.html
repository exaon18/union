NASA data in ASTRA HEALTH

The crew in this prototype is simulated, but the thresholds behind the lab alerts and the solar events behind the radiation alerts are real NASA data: three studies from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) and the NASA DONKI space-weather database. This page shows exactly what we took from each source and how it is used.

How DONKI drives the radiation alerts

DONKI eventCrew effect in ASTRA
SEP (solar energetic particles)Critical for 48 h: dose and cabin radiation red, "shelter in the storm shelter"
X-class flareCritical for 24 h: X flares are the usual source of particle storms
M-class flareCaution for 6 h: watch for particles, limit time in lightly shielded modules
CME ≥ 1000 km/sCaution for 24 h
Geomagnetic storm (Kp)Info only: affects Earth and low orbit, not a crew in deep space

In the "Random / realistic" scenario a real event in the time window raises everyone's radiation automatically. In the separate Simulation Lab (a testing tool, not part of the crew system) any past event can be replayed. Every radiation alert then names the DONKI event ID and time. Limit: DONKI tells us when and how strong, not our crew's dose, so dose values remain simulated.

Limits read before trusting a number

  • Inspiration4 had 4 civilians for 3 days in low Earth orbit; OSD-53 had 6 Shuttle astronauts. Small samples: these bands are for a demo, not clinical reference ranges.
  • "Return day" values mix spaceflight with re-entry and landing stress, so they are an upper estimate of what a long transit would do day to day.
  • Urine values are relative (NPQ, log₂) and only comparable within the same assay.
  • Vitals and lab values for our crew are simulated; only the bands, the gene context and the space-weather events are real.
